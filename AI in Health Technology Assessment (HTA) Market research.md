The Growing Importance of Generative AI in HTA
Health Technology Assessment plays a critical role in determining the clinical effectiveness, economic value, and societal impact of new healthcare technologies before reimbursement and market access decisions are made. Traditionally, HTA requires multidisciplinary teams to review thousands of scientific publications, clinical trial results, patient outcomes, cost-effectiveness models, and healthcare utilization data.

Generative AI is significantly enhancing these activities by automating literature searches, summarizing clinical evidence, generating draft assessment reports, identifying relevant studies, supporting indirect treatment comparisons, and assisting with health economic modeling. AI-powered platforms can rapidly analyze structured and unstructured healthcare data while improving consistency across evidence generation workflows.

Rather than replacing HTA experts, Generative AI enables researchers, clinicians, and health economists to focus on higher-value scientific interpretation and strategic decision-making while reducing administrative workload.

Industry Trends Driving Market Development
Healthcare organizations are increasingly integrating Generative AI into evidence generation platforms, regulatory intelligence systems, and market access workflows. Large language models are being combined with clinical databases, electronic health records (EHRs), scientific literature repositories, and real-world evidence platforms to improve the efficiency of HTA activities.

The industry is also witnessing growing adoption of AI-assisted systematic literature reviews, automated evidence synthesis, value dossier preparation, and pharmacoeconomic modeling. Advances in explainable AI, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and domain-specific healthcare language models are further improving the accuracy and transparency of AI-generated outputs.

In parallel, regulatory agencies and HTA organizations are exploring governance frameworks for responsible AI adoption, emphasizing transparency, validation, human oversight, and data security throughout the assessment process.

Challenges and Technology Considerations
Despite significant technological progress, implementing Generative AI within HTA requires careful validation to ensure scientific accuracy, transparency, and regulatory compliance. AI-generated outputs must be supported by high-quality clinical evidence and reviewed by domain experts before informing reimbursement or policy decisions.

Organizations must also address challenges related to data privacy, bias in AI models, explainability, intellectual property, and interoperability with existing healthcare information systems. As regulatory expectations evolve, human oversight and traceable evidence generation will remain essential for maintaining confidence in AI-assisted HTA processes.

Future development is expected to focus on domain-specific healthcare foundation models, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), multimodal evidence analysis, and AI systems capable of supporting collaborative decision-making across clinical, economic, and regulatory stakeholders.
